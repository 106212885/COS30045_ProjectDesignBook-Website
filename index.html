AUSTRALIAN ROAD SAFETY REPORT • 2011–2021

Beyond the Fatality Count

Shifting the Focus from Deaths to Morbidity

Road-safety debate usually centres on deaths, but most people injured in a crash survive. Those people are easy to overlook. In 2021 alone, Australian data recorded 39,505 hospitalisations from road crashes. This project visualises non-fatal injuries, measures their severity, and compares states fairly to help policymakers decide where prevention efforts are needed most.

02 / QUESTION 1: OVERALL TREND

The National Trend is Not the Same for Everyone

Between 2011 and 2021, hospitalisations rose from 34,033 to 39,505. However, this national total hides divergent trends: pedal cyclists rose by ~51% while pedestrians fell by ~15%.

03 / QUESTION 2: SEVERITY

Volume vs. Severity: A Critical Difference

How many people are hurt and how badly they are hurt are different things. Pedestrians are a small share of total cases, but they stay an average of 9.1 days in hospital compared to 2.9 days for pedal cyclists.

05 / METHODOLOGY

Data Quality & Limitations

01

Inclusion Breaks

Victoria stopped counting emergency-department-only cases in July 2012, and NSW did the same in June 2017. Counts before and after these dates are not fully comparable.

02

Incomplete Mortality Data

"Died cases" are recorded as 0 in every 2021 row, which appears to be missing data. Therefore, mortality analysis is strictly constrained to 2011–2020.
