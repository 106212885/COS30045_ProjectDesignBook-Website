AUSTRALIAN ROAD SAFETY REPORT • 2011–2021

Beyond the Fatality Count

Shifting the Focus from Deaths to Morbidity

Road-safety debate usually centres on fatalities, yet thousands more survive and spend days in hospital—with 39,505 hospitalisations recorded in 2021 alone (410,884 across 2011–2021). This visualisation tracks non-fatal injury trends, compares trauma severity, and adjusts state rates per 100,000 people to guide targeted road-safety prevention.

01 / PURPOSE & AUDIENCE

Answering Three Key Questions

Designed for road-safety policymakers, public-health analysts, and the general public, this visualisation explores 86,140 traffic crash records from BITRE and ABS population data (2011–2021) to answer three critical questions.

01

10-Year Trends (G1)

How have hospitalisations from road crashes changed between 2011 and 2021, and which road user groups are rising or falling?

02

Injury Severity (G2)

Which road user groups have the most serious injuries, using average bed days per case and the share of cases that end in death?

03

State Comparisons (G3)

How do states and territories compare using hospitalisations per 100,000 people once population size is taken into account?

02 / QUESTION 1: CHANGE OVER TIME BY ROAD USER GROUP

G1: Who is being hospitalised? Trends by road user, 2011–2021

Yearly hospitalisations rose from 34,033 in 2011 to a peak of 39,866 in 2019, dipped to 37,966 in 2020, and reached 39,505 in 2021. However, a single national total hides divergent trends: pedal cyclists rose by ~51% (5,391 to 8,163) and motorcyclists by ~17%, while pedestrians fell by ~15% and bus and heavy transport fell by ~17%.

03 / QUESTION 2: SERIOUSNESS OF INJURIES BY ROAD USER

G2: Pedestrians stay longest and are most likely to die

How many people are hurt and how badly they are hurt are different things. Car occupants make up the biggest share of cases (46.9% in 2021), but pedestrians suffer the most serious injuries—staying an average of 9.1 bed days per case with 2.1% of cases ending in death (2011–2020), compared with 2.9 bed days and 0.19% for pedal cyclists.

04 / QUESTION 3: STATES COMPARED BY POPULATION

G3: Hospitalisations per 100,000 people by state

A big state will almost always report more raw cases than a small one. Joining BITRE state hospitalisations with ABS Table 4 resident population reveals that in 2021, the Northern Territory recorded 286.1 hospitalisations per 100,000 people—more than double New South Wales (111.2 per 100,000).

05 / DATA GOVERNANCE, QUALITY & ETHICS

Data Quality, Limitations & Ethical Framing

Cleaned and validated in KNIME across 86,140 Traffic rows against published BITRE Table 1 totals (zero discrepancy across all 11 years), our analysis accounts for four critical data constraints.

01 • SERIES BREAKS

VIC (2012) & NSW (2017) Rule Changes

Victoria stopped counting emergency-department-only cases from 1 July 2012 (lowering national cases by ~2.8% in 2012), and NSW did the same from June 2017. Figures before and after these dates are not fully comparable.

02 • MORTALITY DATA

Incomplete 2020–2021 Death Records

"Died cases" (death within 30 days of admission) fell from 194 in 2019 to 88 in 2020 and 0 in every 2021 row. Because 2021 mortality is incomplete (`died_data_valid = N`), G2 mortality analysis is strictly constrained to 2011–2020.

03 • STATE & RATE SCOPE

Crude Rates & 30-Day Death Exclusions

The BITRE state file excludes patients who died within 30 days (e.g., 33,553 state cases vs 34,033 national cases in 2011) and suppresses small counts as "n.p.". All population rates are crude rates using ABS June quarter estimates, unadjusted for age or sex.

04 • ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS

First Nations Data & Non-Blame Framing

We did not chart the separate BITRE First Nations dataset because matching population denominators were unavailable to compute fair rates, and raw counts can misrepresent risk. Differences across states and road users highlight systemic prevention needs, not individual fault.
